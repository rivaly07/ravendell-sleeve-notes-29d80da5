# Drafts for "Sleeve Notes for the Frog Trio"

## Candidate 1
**Title:** Green Blue
**Blurb:** At the Lily Pad Club, the trio settles into a Tuesday residency. Webster's tenor breathes like marsh grass in wind — long, deliberate phrases that resolve where you don't expect. Mott anchors the bottom on upright, fingers finding the wood's grain. Brushes on the ride, a soft shuffle that never rushes. They play "Round Midnight" at 2 a.m. and the room holds its breath. No novelty. Just three players who listen harder than most.

## Candidate 2
**Title:** Pond Life
**Blurb:** The Green Note, basement level, damp walls that drink the sound. Webster on alto, Mott on bass, Croak on drums — names the regulars know, not punchlines. Their "Blue in Green" stretches past midnight, each chorus peeling a layer off the standard. Mott's solo walks the changes like he's memorized every stone beneath the surface. Croak barely touches the snare; the music breathes in the spaces. You forget the species by the second set.

## Candidate 3
**Title:** Reeds & Roots
**Blurb:** Down at the Bullrush, the trio's Thursday night gig draws the same corner booths. Webster's soprano sings through the smoke — not a gimmick, a voice. Mott plucks the gut strings with calloused fingertips, each note full-bodied and deliberate. Croak's brushes whisper across the heads, riding the cymbal like tide on reeds. They don't play "frog jazz." They play jazz. The difference is in the listening.

## Candidate 4
**Title:** Three on a Log

Croak colors. No one speaks until the last note decays.
